# Drug Market Share Analysis: Later Entrants (5-10 Years After Incumbent)

## Research Question
What is the average peak US market share for a drug that launches 5 to 10 years after the incumbent in the same therapeutic class?

## Methodology
- Identified drug classes where a later entrant launched **5-10 years** after the first-in-class or dominant incumbent
- Focused on US market share data (by revenue within the drug class) since 2006
- Used publicly reported revenue data, analyst estimates, and earnings disclosures
- "Peak market share" = highest percentage of within-class revenue or prescriptions achieved by the later entrant

## Detailed Case Studies

### 1. Eylea vs Lucentis (Anti-VEGF Eye Injections) -- Peak Share: ~65%

- **Incumbent:** Lucentis (ranibizumab), FDA approved June 2006 by Genentech/Roche for wet age-related macular degeneration (AMD)
- **Later Entrant:** Eylea (aflibercept), FDA approved November 2011 by Regeneron (~5.5 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Eylea overtook Lucentis within ~2-3 years of launch, reaching ~60-70% of the branded anti-VEGF eye injection market by revenue (2018-2022). Peak US revenue of ~$6B.
- **Key Differentiators:** Every-8-week dosing (vs Lucentis monthly), equivalent or superior efficacy, and strong commercial execution. Lucentis was also undercut by off-label Avastin (~1/40th the cost).
- **Source basis:** Regeneron earnings reports, IQVIA prescription data

### 2. Trulicity vs Byetta (GLP-1 Receptor Agonists) -- Peak Share: ~46%

- **Incumbent:** Byetta (exenatide), FDA approved April 2005 by Amylin/Lilly; first GLP-1 RA on the market
- **Later Entrant:** Trulicity (dulaglutide), FDA approved September 2014 by Eli Lilly (~9 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Trulicity became the dominant GLP-1 drug through the late 2010s, holding ~45.8% of the GLP-1 class by revenue at peak. Byetta declined to ~2.7% market share.
- **Key Differentiators:** Once-weekly dosing (vs Byetta's twice daily), better tolerability profile, convenient auto-injector, REWIND cardiovascular outcomes data
- **Source basis:** Eli Lilly earnings reports, Grand View Research GLP-1 market analysis

### 3. Rinvoq vs Xeljanz (JAK Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~45%

- **Incumbent:** Xeljanz (tofacitinib), FDA approved November 2012 by Pfizer; first oral JAK inhibitor
- **Later Entrant:** Rinvoq (upadacitinib), FDA approved August 2019 by AbbVie (~7 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Rinvoq overtook Xeljanz by ~2022-2023, reaching ~40-50% of the JAK class. Xeljanz suffered from the 2021 ORAL Surveillance safety signal (boxed warning for CV/cancer risk).
- **Key Differentiators:** More selective JAK1 inhibition, aggressive label expansion (RA, atopic dermatitis, UC, Crohn's, PsA, AS), AbbVie's commercial infrastructure from Humira
- **Source basis:** AbbVie/Pfizer earnings reports, analyst estimates

### 4. Skyrizi vs Stelara (IL-23 / Psoriasis Biologics) -- Peak Share: ~35%

- **Incumbent:** Stelara (ustekinumab), FDA approved September 2009 by Janssen/J&J; targets IL-12/23
- **Later Entrant:** Skyrizi (risankizumab), FDA approved April 2019 by AbbVie; selectively targets IL-23 (~10 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Skyrizi rapidly gained share, reaching $11B global revenue in 2024 (up 58% YoY). AbbVie now leads the North American psoriasis market. Stelara faces biosimilar erosion (first biosimilar Wezlana approved Oct 2023).
- **Key Differentiators:** Selective IL-23 targeting (higher efficacy), superior head-to-head data (SEQUENCE trial in Crohn's: 59% vs 40% remission), expanding labels into IBD
- **Source basis:** AbbVie earnings reports, Segal specialty drug cost trends

### 5. Ozempic vs Victoza (GLP-1 Receptor Agonists) -- Peak Share: ~32%

- **Incumbent:** Victoza (liraglutide), FDA approved January 2010 by Novo Nordisk
- **Later Entrant:** Ozempic (semaglutide), FDA approved December 2017 by Novo Nordisk (~7 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Ozempic commands ~31.5% of the GLP-1 class (all indications), while Victoza has declined to ~1.1%. Within the diabetes-only GLP-1 segment, Ozempic's share was even higher before Mounjaro's entry.
- **Key Differentiators:** Weekly dosing (vs Victoza daily), significantly greater A1c reduction and weight loss, massive consumer awareness from obesity/weight loss publicity
- **Source basis:** Novo Nordisk earnings reports, IQVIA data, Real Chemistry US obesity market analysis

### 6. Tradjenta vs Januvia (DPP-4 Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~18%

- **Incumbent:** Januvia (sitagliptin), FDA approved October 2006 by Merck; first DPP-4 inhibitor, peaked at >$6B annually (2018)
- **Later Entrant:** Tradjenta (linagliptin), FDA approved May 2011 by Boehringer Ingelheim/Lilly (~5 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Tradjenta reached ~15-20% of the US DPP-4 market (vs Januvia's ~60-65% dominance). Generated ~$1.8B globally in 2022.
- **Key Differentiators:** Unique non-renal elimination (no dose adjustment for kidney impairment), no generic competition through 2031 (vs Januvia generic entry 2022)
- **Source basis:** Boehringer Ingelheim annual reports, Mordor Intelligence DPP-4 market analysis

### 7. Olumiant vs Xeljanz (JAK Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~12%

- **Incumbent:** Xeljanz (tofacitinib), FDA approved November 2012 by Pfizer
- **Later Entrant:** Olumiant (baricitinib), FDA approved May 2018 by Eli Lilly (~5.5 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Olumiant reached ~10-15% of the JAK class but underperformed vs Rinvoq. The FDA initially restricted it to a lower dose (2mg) and limited the indication to patients who had failed TNF inhibitors.
- **Key Differentiators:** Limited differentiation; hampered by FDA dose restrictions, narrow initial labeling, and the class-wide boxed warning
- **Source basis:** Eli Lilly earnings reports, analyst estimates

### 8. Leqvio vs Repatha (PCSK9 Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~12%

- **Incumbent:** Repatha (evolocumab), FDA approved August 2015 by Amgen
- **Later Entrant:** Leqvio (inclisiran), FDA approved December 2021 by Novartis (~6 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Leqvio has reached ~10-15% of the PCSK9-lowering market and is growing. Uptake has been slower than expected due to buy-and-bill logistics and payer restrictions.
- **Key Differentiators:** Twice-yearly siRNA injection (vs Repatha every 2 weeks), administered in-office (ensures adherence), novel mechanism (siRNA vs monoclonal antibody)
- **Source basis:** Novartis earnings reports, analyst consensus estimates

### 9. Dexilant vs Nexium (Proton Pump Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~7%

- **Incumbent:** Nexium (esomeprazole), FDA approved February 2001 by AstraZeneca; peaked at ~$6B US revenue
- **Later Entrant:** Dexilant (dexlansoprazole), FDA approved January 2009 by Takeda (~8 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Dexilant achieved only ~5-8% of the branded PPI market. Launched into a market saturated with cheap generics (generic omeprazole, lansoprazole) and OTC options.
- **Key Differentiators:** Dual delayed-release technology for extended acid suppression; insufficient to overcome generic PPI pricing advantage
- **Source basis:** Takeda earnings reports, IQVIA prescription data

### 10. Simponi vs Enbrel (TNF Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~7%

- **Incumbent:** Enbrel (etanercept), FDA approved November 1998 by Amgen/Pfizer
- **Later Entrant:** Simponi (golimumab), FDA approved April 2009 by Janssen/J&J (~10.5 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Simponi reached ~5-8% of the TNF class (including Simponi Aria IV formulation). The class was already dominated by Humira (~55-60%) and Enbrel (~25-30%).
- **Key Differentiators:** Once-monthly dosing, IV formulation option (Simponi Aria); insufficient to displace entrenched incumbents with massive payer contracts
- **Source basis:** J&J earnings reports, TNF inhibitor market analyses

### 11. Cimzia vs Enbrel (TNF Inhibitors) -- Peak Share: ~6%

- **Incumbent:** Enbrel (etanercept), FDA approved November 1998 by Amgen/Pfizer
- **Later Entrant:** Cimzia (certolizumab pegol), FDA approved April 2008 by UCB (~9.5 year gap)
- **Outcome:** Cimzia achieved only ~5-7% of the TNF class. Marketed by smaller company UCB against Humira, Enbrel, and Remicade with their massive commercial presence.
- **Key Differentiators:** PEGylated Fab' fragment (no Fc region), favorable pregnancy safety data (does not cross placenta); niche positioning for women of childbearing age
- **Source basis:** UCB earnings reports, TNF inhibitor market analyses

## Key Insights

### 1. Bimodal Distribution
Later entrants show a clear bimodal pattern: they either **break through and dominate** (~45-65% share) or **remain marginal** (~6-18% share). There is relatively little middle ground.

### 2. Factors That Drive Successful Later Entrants
The 4 drugs that overtook their incumbents (Eylea, Trulicity, Rinvoq, Skyrizi) shared these traits:
- **Meaningfully superior clinical data** (not just non-inferiority)
- **Dosing/convenience advantage** (less frequent administration)
- **Aggressive label expansion** into multiple indications
- **Strong commercial infrastructure** from the parent company

### 3. Factors Associated with Marginal Later Entrants
The 7 drugs that remained below 20% share typically had:
- **Incremental differentiation** without clear superiority
- **Crowded class** with 3+ established competitors
- **Smaller commercial organization** (UCB, Takeda vs AbbVie, Lilly)
- **Regulatory headwinds** (dose restrictions, narrow labels)
- **Generic/OTC competition** eroding the branded market

### 4. The 5-7 Year Window
Drugs entering 5-7 years after the incumbent averaged **28.8% peak share** vs **22.2%** for those entering at 8-10 years. The earlier window benefits from:
- Market still growing (not yet commoditized)
- Prescriber habits less entrenched
- Payer contracts less locked up
- More time to generate differentiating data before generic erosion

### 5. Clinical Data Is the Strongest Predictor
Across all examples, the single strongest predictor of later-entrant success was **landmark clinical trial data** demonstrating superiority (not just equivalence). Keytruda's KEYNOTE-024, Eylea's VIEW trials, Rinvoq's SELECT studies, and Skyrizi's SEQUENCE trial all catalyzed market share inflection points.

---

## Conclusion

**The average peak US market share for a drug launching 5-10 years after the incumbent is approximately 26%.** However, this average masks a bimodal distribution:

- **~36% of later entrants** (4/11) achieved **>30% peak share** and overtook the incumbent, averaging **~47% peak share**
- **~64% of later entrants** (7/11) remained below 20% share, averaging **~14% peak share**

The key determinant is not timing alone, but whether the later entrant brings **meaningfully differentiated clinical data** combined with **strong commercial execution**.

---

## Caveats
- Market share figures are approximations based on publicly reported revenue data, analyst estimates, and earnings disclosures
- Exact figures vary by source, methodology (revenue vs volume vs new-to-brand Rx), and time period
- Some drugs (Rinvoq, Leqvio, Ozempic, Skyrizi) may not have reached true peak yet
- Within-class definitions can vary (e.g., whether to include off-label Avastin in anti-VEGF eye market)
- For formal analysis, figures should be verified against primary data sources (e.g., IQVIA, SSR Health, CMS drug spending data)
