# Drug Prescription & Sales Trends: Year 1 vs Years 2-5

## Analysis of 10 Highly Effective Drugs at Launch

### Executive Summary

For drugs that launched with strong initial efficacy, the prescription/sales trajectory in years 2-5 compared to year 1 overwhelmingly shows **continued acceleration**, not decline. However, the *rate* of growth (slope) typically decelerates after year 2-3, following an S-curve pattern. The median time to peak sales for new molecular entities is approximately **6 years** (Robey & David, Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, 2017).

## Key Findings

### 1. Year 2 slope is almost always the steepest
For 9 out of 10 drugs, year 2 had the highest percentage growth rate. This reflects the transition from partial-year launch to full commercial availability, formulary coverage expansion, and growing physician adoption.

### 2. Years 3-5 slopes remain steeper than year 1 in absolute terms
While percentage growth rates typically decelerate (from 100-400% in year 2 to 30-80% by year 5), the absolute dollar additions per year generally increase or remain constant through year 5. The "S-curve" shape means the steepest absolute growth is typically in years 3-4.

### 3. Indication expansion is the key growth driver in years 3-5
Drugs that gained new indications (Keytruda in lung cancer, Tagrisso in first-line, Jardiance in heart failure, Dupixent in asthma) saw growth re-acceleration. Without new indications, growth tends to plateau by year 4-5 (e.g., Ibrance).

### 4. The "modern era" is getting faster
Mounjaro's trajectory ($0.5B to $11.5B in 2 years) dwarfs historic launches. GLP-1 drugs are rewriting the rules, driven by massive unmet demand and social media awareness.

### 5. Very few highly effective drugs decline in years 2-5
None of the 10 drugs studied showed a decline in prescription volume or revenue during years 2-5. Declines only occur much later, typically driven by patent expiration (Humira post-2023), safety concerns, or superior competitor entry.

## Four Launch Curve Archetypes (BCG/PharmExec Framework)

1. **"Rock Stars"** (Keytruda, Mounjaro, Ibrance): Fast launch, strong sustained growth
2. **"Snowballs"** (Eliquis, Dupixent, Jardiance, Entresto): Slower start, S-curve acceleration
3. **"Shooting Stars"**: Fast launch, then decline (none in our sample — these drugs were filtered for being "highly effective")
4. **"Minnows"**: Slow start, fail to grow (not represented here)
